# Module 70: Founder Brand vs Product Brand

- Status: complete from available evidence
- Framework: founder-brand and studio/product-brand tension
- Participant scope: one founder, Christopher; no additional stakeholder views are on record.

## Raw

### Confirmed founder directives

- OpenScreenReader should be a free, open-source portfolio project.
- It should practically help users and demonstrate that accessibility, privacy, and high-quality local AI can work together.
- No founder biography, diagnosis, personal audience membership, public-persona goal, or preferred level of founder visibility was supplied.

### Existing project evidence

- Project documentation consistently speaks in product terms and defines auditable requirements, boundaries, and validation steps.
- Open source is part of the value proposition, not merely a distribution mechanism.

## Synthesis

### Recommended relationship

Use a **product-first, founder-transparent** model. OpenScreenReader should own the promise, interaction, and trust contract. Christopher can be visibly accountable as the builder and explain decisions, but the brand should not depend on his biography, personal authority, or continuous public performance.

This recommendation preserves the portfolio value while making the project legible as a useful open-source product that contributors and users can evaluate independently.

### Three candidate founder/product models

1. **Product-first, founder-transparent — recommended.** Product identity leads; Christopher is clearly credited as creator and documents decisions and learnings.
2. **Founder-endorsed product.** Christopher appears regularly in demos, technical notes, and release communication while the product retains its own identity.
3. **Public build journal.** The portfolio and learning process become prominent, with OpenScreenReader as the central case study.

Model 3 can support portfolio goals but risks making users feel like test subjects in someone else’s demonstration.

### Brand boundaries

- Do not invent or imply personal disability, diagnosis, professional credential, or lived experience.
- Do not use the founder as evidence that the product meets diverse accessibility needs.
- Let tests, transparent architecture, external feedback, and source inspection carry technical and privacy claims.
- Separate personal opinions from project commitments when publishing under the OpenScreenReader name.
- Make contribution and governance expectations clear if a community forms.

### Projective check: brand without founder presence

The current purpose and product promise remain coherent without a founder story, which supports a durable product brand. The founder adds accountability, craft narrative, and portfolio context rather than supplying the user-facing reason to exist.

### Productive tensions

- Portfolio visibility versus a calm, user-centered utility brand.
- Founder control versus credible open-source participation and succession.
- Transparent building-in-public versus privacy/security communication that avoids premature claims.

### Open decisions

- Choose how prominently Christopher’s name, portrait, and voice appear on the website, repository, demos, and release materials.
- Decide whether progress updates live under Christopher’s personal voice or the OpenScreenReader product voice.
- Define contribution governance, maintainer succession, and decision rights before inviting broad participation.
- Decide whether portfolio case-study content belongs inside the product site or in a separate founder-owned channel.
