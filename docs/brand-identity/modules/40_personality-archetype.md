# Module 40: Personality & Archetype

- Status: complete from available evidence
- Frameworks: Mark & Pearson archetypes, Aaker brand-personality dimensions, projective personification
- Limitation: This module describes the product brand, not Christopher’s personality.

## Raw

### Confirmed founder and project signals

- The app should feel like an unobtrusive, reliable macOS system function.
- Users should retain full control of their data.
- Open source should make privacy claims inspectable.
- The experience should be natural, simple, local, and immediately cancellable.
- Product documents repeatedly favor precise limitations and testable claims over hype, including the explicit boundary that this is not a complete VoiceOver replacement.

No founder adjectives, personal biography, visual preferences, or customer descriptions were supplied.

## Synthesis

### Recommended archetype blend

- **Primary: Caregiver, expressed as respectful enablement.** The brand reduces reading friction and protects sensitive content. It should help without patronizing, diagnosing, or presenting itself as a savior.
- **Secondary: Sage, expressed as verifiability.** Open source, documented data flows, measurable performance, and explicit limits support informed user control.
- **Supporting trait: Creator.** The project demonstrates a better integration of local AI, native interaction, and accessibility, but novelty should remain subordinate to usefulness.

This blend is inferred from product principles; it is not a recorded founder selection.

### Aaker dimensions

- **Competence: high.** Reliable, native, immediate, measurable, technically candid.
- **Sincerity: high.** Transparent, privacy-respecting, open, modest about limitations.
- **Sophistication: moderate.** Natural voice quality and polished macOS behavior, without luxury cues.
- **Excitement: restrained.** Innovation is present, but the desired experience is calm rather than showy.
- **Ruggedness: not relevant.** Avoid forcing this dimension into the identity.

### Behavioral personality

OpenScreenReader should feel calm, capable, respectful, transparent, and quietly modern. It should never feel intrusive, paternalistic, surveillance-driven, breathlessly “AI-powered,” or defensive.

### Three candidate personality formulations

1. **The quiet advocate:** helpful, protective of boundaries, and present only when invited.
2. **The transparent expert:** technically capable, candid about trade-offs, and easy to trust because claims can be checked.
3. **The native craftsperson:** polished, focused, and committed to making advanced local technology feel ordinary.

The recommended identity combines 1 and 2, with 3 guiding product craft.

### Projective personification

If the brand were a person entering a room, the evidence supports someone who does not take the stage: they listen, solve one concrete problem, explain exactly what happens to the data, and leave control with the user. This is a document-based projection.

### Productive tensions

- Caring language can become patronizing if it assumes vulnerability or diagnosis.
- Technical transparency can become dense and undermine the system-like simplicity.
- “Quiet” can become forgettable unless paired with a distinctive promise and visual system.

### Open decisions

- Validate the Caregiver/Sage blend with Christopher and representative users.
- Choose the desired balance between warmth and technical restraint.
- Establish visual attributes; no evidence currently supports specific colors, typography, symbols, or imagery.
- Decide whether the brand should ever use humor; current evidence only supports a restrained default.
