- Status: complete from available evidence

## Raw

### Existing project evidence

`docs/product-vision.md` names these audiences:

- people with reading strain, dyslexia, or ADHD who do not need a complete screenreader;
- knowledge workers listening to short passages from PDFs, presentations, tickets, chats, or videos;
- privacy-conscious users in research, medicine, law, or companies;
- German- and English-speaking users, with language learners as a later audience.

The first MVP focuses on German and English and on short visible screen text, not document libraries or complete system navigation (`docs/ux-requirements.md`).

### Confirmed founder boundaries

- No personal diagnosis or founder biography has been supplied and none should be inferred.
- Privacy, local processing, simplicity, natural speech, free access, and open source are confirmed priorities.

## Synthesis

### Recommended initial niche

**Provisional primary audience:** privacy-conscious Mac users with recurring reading friction who want to listen to short passages across applications without adopting a full screenreader or cloud document workflow.

This is a strategic inference from the overlap of documented audiences, not an approved founder choice.

### Ideal-customer profile

- Uses a Mac and encounters useful text in formats that are awkward to copy or move into another tool.
- Benefits from listening because of reading effort, attention, preference, or workflow—not necessarily a disclosed diagnosis.
- Values a fast, keyboard-led workflow and a natural voice.
- Is unwilling or unable to upload potentially sensitive screen content.
- Wants a focused utility rather than a document ecosystem or complete screenreader.

### Core job, pains, and gains

- **Job:** “When text is visible anywhere on my Mac, help me hear the relevant passage immediately so I can keep moving.”
- **Pains:** copy/paste friction, app boundaries, inaccessible text layers, unnatural system voices, cloud/account requirements, uncertainty about data handling.
- **Gains:** one consistent gesture, natural local speech, immediate stop, transparent permissions, no persistent content history.

### Three candidate audience formulations

1. Mac users who want short on-screen passages read naturally without sending private content to the cloud.
2. People with reading friction who need lightweight listening support, not a full screenreader or document platform.
3. Privacy-sensitive professionals who need trustworthy screen-to-speech across PDFs, presentations, tickets, chats, and visual interfaces.

Candidate 1 is the broadest viable launch formulation; candidates 2 and 3 are potential focused campaign segments.

### Projective check: concrete day-in-the-life

A representative evidence-based use case is selecting a paragraph inside a PDF, slide, ticket, chat, or paused video and hearing it immediately while remaining in the current app. This is a synthesized use case from project documents, not a customer testimonial.

### Productive tensions

- The documented audience is broad, while credible early positioning benefits from one primary niche.
- Accessibility relevance is strong, but the product must not imply full screenreader coverage.
- Professional privacy use cases may suggest enterprise expectations that a free portfolio project does not initially meet.

### Open decisions

- Select and validate the primary launch segment before public release.
- Determine whether accessibility need, privacy need, or general productivity leads acquisition messaging.
- Validate workflows with actual users, including people with dyslexia, ADHD, low vision, or other reading-access needs, without treating diagnoses as a monolith.
- Decide whether regulated professional contexts are examples only or an actively supported niche with corresponding assurance needs.
